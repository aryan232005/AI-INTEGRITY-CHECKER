Vertical integration is a strategy in which a company takes ownership of several stages of its production and distribution chain rather than buying those stages from independent firms. Backward integration moves a firm toward its raw materials and suppliers, while forward integration moves it toward its customers, for example by opening its own retail outlets. Firms pursue it to secure supply, capture the margin earned by intermediaries, and protect proprietary know-how. The costs are significant: large capital commitments, reduced flexibility when technology or demand shifts, and the managerial difficulty of running businesses with very different economics. Economists often explain the trade-off through transaction costs. When contracts are hard to write or enforce, owning the activity is cheaper than negotiating for it repeatedly. When markets are competitive and standards are clear, buying from specialists is usually the more efficient choice.
