Working capital is the difference between a company's current assets and its current liabilities, and it measures the short-term funds available to run daily operations. The cash conversion cycle combines the days inventory is held, the days customers take to pay, and the days the company takes to pay suppliers. Shortening the cycle frees cash without any new financing. Fast-growing firms often run short of working capital even while profitable, because they must buy inventory and extend credit before customers pay.
