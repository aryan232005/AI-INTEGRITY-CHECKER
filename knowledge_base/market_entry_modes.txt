Companies entering a foreign market choose among several modes. Exporting requires little investment but gives limited control over local sales. Licensing lets a local firm produce and sell under the brand in return for royalties, at the risk of leaking know-how. A joint venture shares ownership and risk with a local partner who contributes market knowledge, although disagreements over control are common. A wholly owned subsidiary, built from scratch or acquired, offers the greatest control and the greatest cost. The choice usually depends on the size of the opportunity, the regulatory environment, cultural distance and how quickly the firm needs to move.
