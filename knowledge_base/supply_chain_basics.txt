A supply chain is the network of organisations, people, information and resources involved in moving a product from raw material to the final customer. Core activities include sourcing, manufacturing, warehousing, transportation and returns handling. Performance is commonly judged on cost, delivery reliability, speed and inventory turnover. A frequent problem is the bullwhip effect, where small changes in consumer demand produce increasingly large swings in orders further up the chain. Companies reduce it by sharing sales data with suppliers, shortening lead times and ordering in smaller, more frequent batches. Disruptions such as port closures or supplier failures have pushed many firms to hold safety stock and to qualify second suppliers.
